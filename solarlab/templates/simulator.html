Season by season

What Wh means: watt-hours. A 100 W panel working at full power for one hour makes 100 Wh. A phone battery holds roughly 15 Wh. What the model leaves out: it assumes the sun is straight in front of the way the panel tilts, like a panel that also turns left and right to follow the sun. A real fixed panel gets less light early in the morning and late in the evening, so real daily energy is lower. The numbers are for a place about 28° north of the equator and do not include wiring or battery losses.

Experiment Angle θ Weather Output (W)

The power comes from Lambert's cosine law, P = Pmax × w × I × (1 − s) × cos(θ), for a 100 W panel. Here w is the weather factor (1.0 sunny, 0.6 partly cloudy, 0.25 cloudy), I is the sunlight intensity from 0 to 1 and s is the fraction of the panel in shade (0 unless you include shade). The weather factors are assumed values for the demonstration, not measurements. The colour styles are only inspired by popular websites and are not connected to them.
